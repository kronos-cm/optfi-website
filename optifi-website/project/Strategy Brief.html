/ 00  ·  TL;DR

The seven moves that take the site
from decent to brilliant.

The shortlist

  1. Replace the headline."Disciplined compounding, proven before trusted" is process. Lead with the outcome: "Keep more of what crypto actually compounds."
  2. Strip the operator dialect."Canary execution", "proof window", "walk-forward validation", "signing policy" — language for engineers, not the actual visitor. Replace with plain claims a curious retail reader can repeat to a friend.
  3. Stop projecting authority you haven't earned yet.The current site signals "live system in production". Pre-launch is the truth — and it's a stronger story when told honestly.
  4. Promote the journal to the front.The blog is the acquisition channel. Right now it's invisible. It should be the second thing on the page, not the fifth.
  5. One CTA. Then one CTA again.The hero today offers three equal-weight buttons. Decide what success means. Then make every section point at it.
  6. Build the trust the IP allows.No invented logos, no false canary numbers, no fake testimonials. Trust is earned from the writing, the cadence, and the discipline of saying "we're not open yet."
  7. Commit to a visual point of view.The current pastel-glass-card aesthetic reads generic SaaS. A premium niche financial product deserves a confident one.
/ 01  ·  What the site is doing wrong

A polite version
of an impolite truth.

The current homepage is competent. It is also doing roughly the opposite of what a pre-launch acquisition site needs to do. Here's where, and why.

ISSUE 01

The five-second test fails on language, not design.

The first words a stranger reads are "Tax-Aware Crypto Wealth Engine" followed by "Disciplined compounding, proven before trusted." This is a thesis statement for the team running the project, not a proposition for the person reading it. By the time we say "OptFi builds after-tax crypto returns through a staged proof sequence" we've used four pieces of jargon — and the reader's most charitable thought is "...okay, but what is it?"

Now (homepage hero)
"Tax-Aware Crypto Wealth Engine — Disciplined compounding, proven before trusted."

Three abstract nouns. Zero outcomes. The visitor has to decode this — and they won't.

Proposed
"Keep more of what crypto actually compounds."

A promise the reader can finish in their own head. Tax, fees, slippage are the explanation — but the hook is the only thing the eye needs.

ISSUE 02

Three competing CTAs in the hero — none of them is the goal.

"How the proof sequence works" / "View current status" / "Roadmap". The platform is pre-launch — none of these are conversions. Worse, they implicitly tell the visitor "the platform is the point", which is not true today. The point today is: get them on the waitlist and into the journal. Everything else is a leak.

ISSUE 03

The site signals authority it hasn't yet earned — and risks leaking IP doing it.

Active canary, hardware-wallet signing policy, walk-forward validation, FIFO accounting, proof windows. These are details about how the platform works — the very IP that should stay private. They also create a credibility gap: nothing on the page proves any of it, so the operator-mode confidence reads as overclaim. Pre-launch is the more honest, more compelling story.

Now (Pillars section)
"Safety Gates First. No system can promote itself to live trading without clearing a measured proof window…"

Reveals architectural specifics. Most visitors don't understand it; the ones who do can reverse-engineer the strategy stack.

Proposed (Principles section)
"Evidence opens the door. Live access is gated on measured outcomes — not on dates or optimism."

Same conviction. Zero IP. Easier to read. Stronger to write.

ISSUE 04

The blog isn't on the homepage.

If the journal is the acquisition channel — and it should be, given pre-launch status — it has to be the second-largest surface on the page, after the hero. Right now there is no journal section at all on the homepage. The strategy gap is enormous: every post that ranks on Google lands a stranger on a page that doesn't reinforce why they should subscribe.

ISSUE 05

The visual language reads "SaaS dashboard template" — not "premium financial product."

Translucent glass cards, soft pastel gradients, friendly rounded icons, dotted grid background, sky-blue accents. Each of those is a common move for crypto/web3 SaaS landing pages. None of them convey what a tax-aware, patient, evidence-led product should convey: conviction. The aesthetic should feel closer to a private bank or a hedge-fund letter than a SaaS billboard. Confident type, restrained palette, editorial rhythm. Premium is a feeling — and right now the site signals "another L1 platform pitch" instead of "operator with discipline."

ISSUE 06

"Germany-first" is a unique angle and the site buries it.

Germany has one of the most demanding crypto tax regimes in the world. That is a genuine strategic moat: it is harder, more specific, and more useful to your real audience (DACH retail) than another "decentralised yield" pitch. The current site mentions it inside an FAQ. It should be in the eyebrow, the headline supporting line, and the trust strip. Lead with the constraint that makes you defensible.

ISSUE 07

The FAQ does more positioning work than the rest of the page.

It is the best-written content on the site today. The honesty ("Not yet… DeFi execution is deferred…") is a competitive asset. The mistake is hiding it at the bottom under a Q&A label, where it reads as "objection handling" rather than as "this is how we think." Pull the strongest answers out and let them carry sections in their own right.

/ 02  ·  Repositioning

One sentence positioning, then
everything follows from it.

A landing page is a positioning statement with a layout. Lock the positioning, the layout writes itself.

Positioning statement (working)

The line that should sit behind every page
OptFi is a tax-aware crypto wealth engine, built Germany-first, for people who want their crypto to compound in a way that survives the tax bill — not just the screenshot.

Why it works. "Tax-aware" — specific. "Wealth engine" — outcome-shaped. "Germany-first" — defensible moat. "Compound" — the verb that does the most work in finance. "Survives the tax bill, not just the screenshot" — names the antagonist (gross-yield marketing) without picking a fight.

Audience & jobs-to-be-done

The primary visitor is a tax-aware, crypto-curious, German-speaking retail investor. Skeptical, busy, intelligent. They are not looking for "another yield product." They are looking for someone they can trust to think honestly about a market that has consistently disrespected their intelligence.

Their three jobs on this site, in order: (1) understand whether you're serious in five seconds, (2) read something that proves it, (3) commit to staying in touch. The whole layout exists to walk that staircase.

One conversion. Two surfaces.

The primary action is join the waitlist. The secondary action — which fuels the primary — is subscribe to the journal. Both ask for an email. The waitlist is the promise; the journal is the proof. Every section needs to lean on one or the other. Anything that doesn't is decoration.

/ 03  ·  Homepage architecture

Six sections. Each one
does exactly one job.

The reordering is the strategy. Move the journal up; move the architecture details out; let the conviction carry the rest.

The new section order

  • Hero Outcome headline + 2-sentence promise + waitlist CTA + read-journal CTA. Right column: latest journal entry as a card — signalling instantly that this is also a place to read. Convert / Hook
  • The Thesis A single confident pull-quote and three short paragraphs. No jargon, no IP. Plain claim: crypto's marketing problem is a maths problem; here's the maths we care about. Position
  • The Journal The centerpiece. Featured entry + grid of 4–5 recent posts + category chips + inline newsletter signup. This is the surface that does the acquisition work. Earn trust
  • How we build Four principles, written in plain English. Take-home not headline. Evidence opens the door. Boring on purpose. Public process. No architecture leak. Reassure
  • FAQ Six honest answers. Lead with "Can I use OptFi today?" — answer "No, not yet" with conviction. Honesty is the asset. Disarm
  • Waitlist A single big hero-sized closing block. One field, one button. Three short bullets of what the visitor gets. End on conviction, not on a form. Convert

/ 05  ·  The conversion checklist

What gets kept,
what gets cut.

A one-page reference for whoever ships the changes. Tape it above the monitor.

Keep / Add

  • Outcome-led headline as the first words on the page
  • Featured journal card visible in the hero
  • "Germany-first" called out in the eyebrow and the thesis
  • Pull-quote framing the thesis without jargon
  • Journal as section 2 — categorised, with newsletter signup inline
  • "Pre-launch · Waitlist open" pill in the nav
  • One closing waitlist block — generous size, one input, one button
  • FAQ that leads with "Can I use OptFi today?" → no
  • Plain-English principles (no architecture talk)
  • Single conviction sentence in the footer ("Patience over performance.")

Cut / Replace

  • "Disciplined compounding, proven before trusted" as the headline
  • Three equal-weight hero buttons
  • "Canary execution / Paper trading / Live compounding" stage cards — IP leak
  • "Hardware-wallet signing", "walk-forward", "FIFO" anywhere public
  • "Current Platform State" tile with fake/specific status counts
  • Sky-blue + cream glass-card pastel palette
  • Soft rounded SaaS iconography (lucide grids of pillar cards)
  • Anything resembling a fake dashboard with invented numbers
  • Multiple low-energy doc links treated as a "next step"
  • Anything that doesn't lean on waitlist or journal subscribe
/ 06  ·  What comes next

Three weeks
to a brilliant site.

Week 1 — copy & positioning

Lock the positioning sentence. Rewrite the hero, thesis, principles, and FAQ in the institutional voice and outcome-led tone. Draft the first three "anchor" journal entries that the homepage will feature — these double as SEO landing pages.

Week 2 — build & visual

Implement the new homepage in the existing React/Tailwind stack. Replace the design tokens — dark background, signal lime accent, Instrument Serif display, Geist body, Geist Mono utility. Rebuild the few primitive components (Card, Button, Section) to match. The rest of the site (docs, status, roadmap) can carry the new tokens without restructuring.

Week 3 — instrument & iterate

Wire the waitlist and newsletter signups to a real list provider. Add a single source of conversion truth (hero waitlist, closing waitlist, inline newsletter form). Run a one-week light user test with five DACH retail crypto holders — record what they say out loud at the five-second mark. Iterate on that, not on internal taste.

Beyond — the second order benefit

The aesthetic and copy decisions made here travel everywhere: the operator letter, future investor decks, every social post. A homepage isn't a page — it's the highest-volume way the brand teaches the rest of the company how to sound. Decide it on purpose, and most other writing gets easier from this point on.
